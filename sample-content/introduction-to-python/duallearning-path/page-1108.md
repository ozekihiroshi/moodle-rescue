## Teacher answers: Connected transfer challenges

These are reference approaches, not exact-match requirements. Ask learners to run, modify, and explain their solution.

### Lesson 1: Your first Python program

**Challenge:** A course scheduled 24 training hours and delivered 20. Display both values with clear labels.

**Reference:** scheduled = 24; delivered = 20; print both values with labels. Accept separate lines or one readable line.

### Lesson 2: Variables, types, input, and calculations

**Challenge:** A centre has 29 completions from 36 registered learners. Calculate its completion percentage to one decimal place.

**Reference:** 29 / 36 \* 100 gives about 80.6%. The learner should identify registered as the denominator.

### Lesson 3: Decisions with conditions

**Challenge:** Materials below 10 units require reorder; 10–19 require monitoring; 20 or more are sufficient. Test 9, 10, 19, and 20.

**Reference:** Use if stock \< 10, elif stock \< 20, else. The four boundary tests should produce reorder, monitor, monitor, sufficient.

### Lesson 4: Repetition with loops

**Challenge:** Loop through weekly material costs \[82.5, 74.0, 91.5, 80.0\] and report total and highest weekly cost.

**Reference:** The total is 328.0 and the maximum is 91.5. A loop-based maximum or max() is acceptable if explained.

### Lesson 5: Lists and dictionaries

**Challenge:** Add a third centre containing course name, scheduled hours, and delivered hours; print delivery percentage.

**Reference:** Use one dictionary with labelled keys, append it to the list, then calculate delivered / scheduled \* 100.

### Lesson 6: Functions, errors, and testing

**Challenge:** Create cost_per_completion(material_cost, completed) with the same invalid-denominator protection. Test 450/30 and 450/0.

**Reference:** The valid result is 15.0 and the zero-completion result should be None or another explicitly documented invalid result.

### Lesson 7: Tables, CSV, and pandas

**Challenge:** Select centre_name, registered, and completed, then calculate a completion_rate column.

**Reference:** Use a column list for selection and df\["completed"\] / df\["registered"\] \* 100 for the new column.

### Lesson 8: Inspecting and selecting data

**Challenge:** Create a list of centre-months with at least 24 scheduled training hours but fewer than 20 delivered hours.

**Reference:** Combine both Boolean conditions with &, wrap each in parentheses, and select only identifying and hour columns.

### Lesson 9: Cleaning data

**Challenge:** Flag negative material cost, delivered hours above scheduled hours, and duplicate centre-month-course rows. Report each count.

**Reference:** Use comparisons for impossible ranges and duplicated(subset=\[...\], keep=False). Do not silently discard the rows.

### Lesson 10: Grouping and summary statistics

**Challenge:** Group by course and calculate total training hours, total material cost, and cost per completion.

**Reference:** Aggregate hours, cost, and completions by course, then divide each course total cost by its total completions.

### Lesson 11: Visualisation and evidence

**Challenge:** Create a bar chart comparing completion rate by course. Write one observation and one limitation.

**Reference:** Aggregate totals before calculating each course rate. A limitation could be differing course difficulty or small group size.
